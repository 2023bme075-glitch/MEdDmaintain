WHY MAINTENANCE MATTERS

Medical equipment is part of the care team.

Equipment supports diagnosis, monitoring and treatment. A structured maintenance programme helps healthcare facilities:

  • Keep devices safe and fit for their intended use.
  • Identify faults before they cause avoidable interruptions.
  • Maintain reliable measurements and device performance.
  • Document service history for follow-up and accountability.

THE MAINTENANCE CYCLE

Four important parts of equipment care

Maintenance begins before a device enters clinical use and continues throughout its service life.

1. Installation and setup

Prepare equipment for safe use.

  • Check site, power and environmental requirements.
  • Install and configure the device.
  • Test operation before clinical use.

2. Preventive maintenance

Reduce the likelihood of equipment failure.

  • Perform planned inspections and cleaning.
  • Replace worn parts when required.
  • Follow manufacturer recommendations.

3. Calibration and testing

Check accuracy and safe performance.

  • Verify measurements against references.
  • Test alarms and safety functions.
  • Record results and adjustments.

4. Troubleshooting and repair

Restore devices when faults occur.

  • Identify and diagnose the fault.
  • Repair or replace faulty components.
  • Verify performance before return to use.

THE OUTCOME

Better equipment care supports better healthcare.

Consistent maintenance supports reliable devices and helps clinical teams plan around equipment needs.

  • Patient and staff safety
  • Equipment availability
  • Longer useful service life
  • Traceable service history
